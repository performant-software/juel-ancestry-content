The school rented laborers from enslavers who lived across Central Virginia. When the school opened in 1825, the university continued to rent enslaved people from across that same region.               ## Enslavement and Ancestry at UVA and Beyond


              UVA’s Gibbons Project, the successor to two presidential
              commissions researching slavery and the age of segregation at the
              university, is dedicated to developing materials that make
              accessible and intelligible the vast array of archival materials
              identifying those enslaved at UVA and in the surrounding region.
              This digital project represents our effort to answer calls from
              descendants to make documents accessible, to make them legible, to
              make them searchable, and to create something that allows
              descendants and researchers to work forward and backward in time
              as they identify ancestors and build family trees. The materials
              here began with slavery at UVA but will continue to expand to
              Central Virginia and beyond and are not limited to materials that
              are directly related to UVA. Our research team continues to add
              new materials as we find them in the archives.
